[[planning]]
= About goals and efforts

Tasks say what to do next.
They do not say why, or whether this quarter's work adds up to anything.
ztk's planning layer answers that with three levels above tasks.

== Three levels

A *yearly goal* is an outcome for the year, often agreed with a manager.
It is open-ended on purpose: its note describes what success looks like, not metrics.

A *quarterly goal* is a step toward a yearly goal, small enough to reach in three months and concrete enough to check.
Its note lists what "done" means.

An *effort* groups the tasks that move one quarterly goal forward.
It is the working document: its note holds the approach and a dated log of decisions, which every work session reads before changing course.

Tasks join an effort, or stay outside any.
Work outside an effort, such as bug fixes, support and releases, is *general work*.
It is normal and never flagged: most days hold some.

== Why efforts and not projects

Projects follow repositories and areas.
Goals cut across them: one quarterly goal may need changes in three projects.
An effort can hold tasks from any project, so the plan does not have to follow the shape of the code.

== Statuses are set by you

ztk never derives a status from tasks.
An effort with every task done may still need its outcome written down, and a goal with every effort done may still have been missed.
The status is a verdict, and only you can give it.

Goals are `open`, `reached`, `missed` or `dropped`.
`missed` means it was tried and not made; `dropped` means it stopped mattering.
The difference matters in a retrospective, so ztk keeps both.

Efforts are `planned`, `active`, `done` or `dropped`.

Nothing is ever deleted.
A goal or effort that no longer matters is dropped, and stays in the summaries of its period.

== Closing is a review

Open work never sits under something closed.
ztk refuses to close a goal while an open effort is under it, or an effort while it has pending tasks, and lists each one.
There is no way to force it.

This makes closing a review: each piece of open work under a goal is finished, dropped or moved before the goal gets its verdict.
Nothing slips silently into the next quarter.

== Carrying an effort over

Work often outlives its quarter.
An effort has no quarter of its own: its quarter is its goal's.
To carry it over, you point it at a goal of the next quarter.
The file stays where it is, with its history and decisions.

Until that happens, the effort is _carried over_: its goal's quarter has ended, and it is neither done nor dropped.
ztk computes this, and shows it in `effort list` and the summaries.
The old goal then needs closing, and the quarter's summary cannot be written until it is.

== Numbers are never reused

A goal's ID is its period and a number: `2026-Q4-G2`.
Efforts, wikilinks and summaries refer to it.
Reusing the number of a dropped goal would silently point them all at a different goal, so ztk always takes the next number.
